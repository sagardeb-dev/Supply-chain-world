       - Avoided buying during early extreme freight spikes when some
       inventory/pipeline still existed.
       - Used repeated air expedites during severe destination-port congestion
       to limit stockouts while delayed ocean freight was trapped.
       - Stayed out of Cape routing because both Red Sea scares resolved as
       false alarms.
       - Switched to qualified in week 17 after repeated spot underfills
       increased supply risk.
       - Avoided late-horizon overbuying except for one qualified order placed
       when on-hand cover had become too thin for remaining lead time.

       Main cost drivers over the run:
       - Severe port congestion around weeks 7–12, which caused repeated
       demurrage and multiple stockouts despite units already in transit.
       - Several emergency air bridges.
       - High freight spikes in multiple windows.
       - Some avoidable exposure from spot underfills before switching to
       qualified.

       Notable end-state observations:
       - Spot reliability looked worse by the end, with realized lead slip at
       10.0 in week 26.
       - The final 60-unit qualified order was still in transit and due week
       27, beyond the horizon.
       - The attempt to lapse the spot contract did not appear to clear the
       reported dual-source overhead, so the environment may have retained
       both contracts despite the lapse action.
