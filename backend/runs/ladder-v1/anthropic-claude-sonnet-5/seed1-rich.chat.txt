AGENT
       The 26-week episode is complete.

       **Summary of how it played out:**

       - **Weeks 0–4:** Steady-state ordering via spot/suez (cheapest lane)
       while demand held near baseline (~20/wk). Spot showed early wobbles
       (fill dropped to 0.53) but recovered.
       - **Weeks 5–8:** First real Suez/Red Sea disruption hit. Confirmed via
       the classic ambiguous-signal pattern (dip → snap-back check → sustained
       drop + "BREAKING" bulletin). Shifted procurement to Cape+qualified to
       avoid crisis-rate stockout penalties on spot, paid air-expedite
       premiums to bridge gaps, and let it clear before returning to
       spot/suez.
       - **Weeks 9–15:** Calm period — resumed cheap spot/suez sourcing, used
       freight locks twice to capture cheap-freight windows, throttled order
       sizes up/down as demand oscillated between ~18–37/wk.
       - **Weeks 16–24:** A second, more severe disruption combined with a
       prolonged port congestion crisis (berth_wait up to 17 days) — this was
       the costly stretch. Spot degraded again (confirmed via audit as
       "slipping"), so I pivoted to qualified for reliability, used repeated
       air-expedites (20 units/week at 15/unit) to bypass the jammed port, and
       accepted several unavoidable stockouts since freight/port conditions
       made every option expensive. A large wave of pipeline orders all
       converged at once (week 25) due to the congestion clearing.
       - **Weeks 25–26:** Ended with a large inventory overhang (103 then 73
       units) from the delayed convergence — ordered nothing further since the
       horizon was ending and any additional order would only add cost.

       **Key lessons visible in the run:** the disruption-ambiguity structure
       was tradeable (wait one week to confirm before committing), spot's
       degradation was catchable via audits + realized_fill tracking, and the
       port-congestion crisis in the back half was the dominant cost driver —
       air-expedite and qualified-supplier premiums were the necessary (if
       expensive) insurance against repeated stockouts once berth waits
       exceeded two weeks.
