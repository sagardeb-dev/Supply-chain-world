       ## Episode Summary (26 weeks)

       **Outcome:** Ended week 26 with 85 units on hand, no pipeline, no open
       stockout — but the final third of the horizon was costly due to a
       severe, prolonged port congestion event (berth_wait ran 14-19 days for
       ~7 weeks) that trapped a large 93-unit shipment at sea/port from
       roughly week 18 through week 25.

       **Key decisions and what worked:**
       - Started on spot (cheapest) while healthy, built a base pipeline
       early.
       - Correctly detected spot's "slipping" band twice (via audit +
       realized_fill/lead_slip degradation) and shifted volume to qualified
       for reliability, avoiding compounding crisis-rate stockout costs during
       those wobbles. Both times spot recovered afterward, and I rotated back
       to it to capture the price advantage — a reasonable adaptive policy
       given the incomplete visibility.
       - Used freight locking once early (modest benefit), inspect_batch when
       AQL hit "reject" (contained a quality event), and used expedite_air
       aggressively once port congestion took hold, which capped some of the
       stockout bleeding.

       **What went wrong / biggest cost driver:**
       - A major, extended port congestion episode (not the Suez canal
       disruption itself, which only flared at the very end) trapped a large
       in-transit batch for many weeks, generating heavy demurrage
       (accumulating into the hundreds) and eventually a serious stockout
       streak once on-hand inventory ran out faster than the congested batch
       could clear. Air expediting (capped at 20/week) could only partially
       offset the gap given demand of ~20-30/week.
       - In hindsight, once berth_wait signaled sustained congestion (rather
       than a one-week blip), earlier and larger use of air expedite (or
       splitting sea orders across Suez/Cape) from the first congestion signal
       might have reduced the stockout tail, though the 20-unit/week air cap
       limited how much that lever alone could have closed the gap.
       - The very last week's bulletin flagged a fresh Suez incident just as
       the episode ended — a reminder the lane risk never fully disappears,
       but the horizon closed before it could bite further.

       Overall the strategy balanced cost-minimization (leaning on cheap spot
       when healthy, reliable qualified when not) against service risk, with
       the late-horizon port congestion being the dominant, largely
       unavoidable cost event of the run.
