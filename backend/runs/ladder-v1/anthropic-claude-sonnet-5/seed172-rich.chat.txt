========================== WRAP-UP (episode over) ==========================

AGENT
       The episode is complete (26 weeks run).

       **Summary of the run:**

       - **Weeks 0–4:** Steady-state replenishment via Suez/spot at roughly
       demand pace (20-28 units/wk), spot running healthy fill rates.
       - **Weeks 5–11:** Hit a severe destination-port congestion episode
       (berth_wait spiked to 16-19, demurrage compounding to 200+/wk) even
       though the Suez lane itself stayed calm. Responded by pausing new sea
       orders (to avoid feeding the stuck queue), using repeated air-expedites
       (20 units/wk at 15/unit) to bypass the port and keep inventory from
       fully collapsing, plus a freight lock, an inspection, and an
       audit/briefing to diagnose whether it was a lane disruption (it wasn't)
       or a port problem (it was).
       - **Week 12:** The full backed-up pipeline (106 units) landed at once,
       congestion cleared (berth_wait back to ~0-2), restoring a large
       inventory buffer.
       - **Weeks 13–20:** Returned to steady cadence, using freight locks
       opportunistically when the index was cheap (e.g., 0.45x, 0.82x, 0.93x)
       to shield against forecasted spikes, sizing orders (12-25 units) to
       keep ~3-4 weeks of coverage without over-holding.
       - **Weeks 21–23:** Spot's reliability began deteriorating
       (realized_fill fell to 0.47-0.79, audit revealed "slipping"),
       coinciding with a demand uptick (pos_units into the 30s). Used
       inspection once against a "reject" AQL reading and layered in air-
       expedites to cover the gap directly rather than trusting degraded spot
       fills.
       - **Weeks 24–25 (endgame):** With only 1-2 weeks left in the horizon,
       stopped placing new sea orders (a 3-week transit wouldn't arrive in
       time) and relied on air-expedite plus existing on-hand/in-transit stock
       to serve remaining demand, avoiding wasted shipping/holding spend right
       at the close.
       - **Week 26 (final):** Episode ended with inventory at 7, having served
       36 units of demand that week (some unmet demand likely occurred as
       spot's reliability degraded into the final weeks — an unavoidable
       trade-off given no time left to re-source via slower/pricier reliable
       suppliers like qualified or backup).

       **Key decisions that shaped total cost:**
       1. Never switched off spot to qualified/backup despite the mid-run
       degradation — in hindsight, given spot's OTIF band dropped to
       "slipping" by week 25 with realized_fill sub-0.5 in week 23, migrating
       a portion of volume to qualified around week 22-23 (once the audit
       flagged "slipping") could have reduced stockout/air-freight reliance in
       the final weeks.
       2. The congestion episode (weeks 5-12) was the single largest cost
       driver (heavy demurrage + ~7 rounds of air-expedite at 15/unit).
       Pausing sea orders during that window was correct — continuing to pour
       orders into a jammed port would have compounded holding+demurrage
       further.
       3. Freight locks were used opportunistically and generally captured
       cheap-rate windows (0.45x-0.93x) ahead of forecasted spikes, which paid
       off across weeks 13-21.
