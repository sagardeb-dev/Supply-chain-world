AGENT
       The episode is complete — 26 weeks run, ending with the report
       confirming **EPISODE DONE**.

       **Summary of how the horizon played out:**

       - **Weeks 0–2:** Started steady ~20 unit/week replenishment via
       Suez+spot (cheapest lane/supplier), matching baseline demand while
       inventory buffer was ample.
       - **Weeks 2–4:** Detected a genuine Suez disruption signal (bulletin
       escalation + confirmed by paid briefing + a costly 3x crisis backorder
       charge). Pivoted to Cape routing and signed a short-term qualified
       contract as a reliable hedge, avoiding further spot exposure during the
       scare.
       - **Week 4:** Bulletin snapped back to normal, confirming it was a
       false alarm — resumed cheaper spot/Suez sourcing, used freight locks
       opportunistically when spot rate spikes were forecast, and air-
       expedited small batches to cover pipeline gaps.
       - **Weeks 5–17:** Ran a steady-state replenishment loop, sizing orders
       to realized demand and inventory position, using audits/inspections
       when spot's OTIF band or AQL results flagged wobbles
       ("marginal"/"slipping"/"reject"), and locking freight during favorable-
       to-spike transitions.
       - **Weeks 18–23:** A real spot reliability decline emerged (fill rates
       crashing to 0.61–0.85, confirmed "slipping" by audit) alongside
       recurring quality rejects — shifted sourcing to the reliable qualified
       supplier, used air expedite and inspections to control damage, and kept
       the freight lock active to cap shipping costs.
       - **Weeks 23–26 (endgame):** Recognized that new sea orders (3–4 week
       lead times) would not land before the 26-week horizon closed, so
       stopped ordering entirely, let the existing pipeline (qualified
       shipments already in transit) land, and accepted a small terminal
       drawdown in inventory rather than waste spend on unusable orders.

       **Key decisions that shaped total cost:**
       1. Kept spot as default low-cost supplier but pulled back to qualified
       whenever reliability/quality signals turned negative.
       2. Used the buy_briefing/buy_audit levers only during ambiguous risk
       windows (disruption onset, spot degradation) rather than every week,
       avoiding overspend on information.
       3. Used freight locks tactically when the outlook pointed to a spike,
       not on every order.
       4. Used air-expedite and inspections as targeted, temporary fixes
       during the two real stress events (disruption scare, spot degradation)
       rather than routinely.
       5. Correctly stopped ordering in the final weeks once lead times
       exceeded the remaining horizon, avoiding wasted shipping spend at the
       tail end.
