AGENT
       The episode is complete after 26 weeks.

       ## Summary

       The 26-week horizon ran through several distinct phases:

       **Weeks 0-6 (steady state):** Started with 80 units, ran steady
       replenishment (~20-25/wk) via Suez/spot at the cheap spot rate
       (2.5/unit premium below base). Lane and freight were calm.

       **Weeks 6-9 (spot wobble → real disruption):** Spot's audit flagged
       "slipping" with a realized_fill drop to 0.51. I hedged by signing a
       short-term qualified contract and shifting sourcing there. This proved
       prescient — a genuine Suez blockage hit (grounded vessel), confirmed by
       a crashing suez_count/bab_count and an explicit "BREAKING" bulletin. I
       locked freight for 4 weeks (capping the spike at 1.76x rather than the
       peak ~400 index), used Cape routing for new orders to bypass the
       blocked corridor, and leaned on air-expedite repeatedly to bypass port
       congestion that persisted even after the canal reopened.

       **Weeks 10-15 (disruption unwind):** Congestion/demurrage lingered even
       after the canal cleared (berth_wait staying 12-18 days for several
       weeks), so I kept using air-expedite for immediate needs while holding
       off on new ocean orders to avoid compounding an already massive
       121-unit pipeline. This resulted in a big arrival wave (114 units in
       week 15) once the backlog cleared.

       **Weeks 15-24 (recovery to steady state):** Drew down the surplus,
       resumed normal ~20-22 unit/week Suez/spot orders once spot's OTIF and
       realized_fill normalized, using periodic audits and inspect_batch when
       AQL results flagged marginal/reject quality.

       **Weeks 24-25 (wind-down):** Stopped placing new ocean orders since
       ~3-week transit times meant they wouldn't land within the horizon;
       relied on existing pipeline and air-expedite to cover final demand
       without stranding excess inventory or capital.

       **Key decisions that shaped cost:**
       - Early detection of spot's reliability wobble avoided being caught
       fully exposed to spot during the disruption (which carries a 3x
       stockout penalty).
       - The freight lock during the spike weeks meaningfully capped shipping
       cost exposure versus paying spot rates that hit 400+.
       - Air-expedite was used aggressively (7 times, 20 units each at 15/unit
       = ~2,100 total) as insurance against stockouts during the
       disruption/congestion window — expensive per-unit but far cheaper than
       repeated 20/unit stockout penalties, especially the 3x crisis-rate
       stockouts.
       - Held off ordering during the "long pipeline" period (weeks 10-14) to
       avoid stacking further holding/demurrage cost on top of an already deep
       pipeline, accepting temporary stockouts as the lesser cost.
       - Wound down ocean ordering in the final two weeks since transit time
       exceeded the remaining horizon.

       The episode is now finished (`EPISODE DONE`), ending week 26 with 34
       units on hand and no open pipeline.
