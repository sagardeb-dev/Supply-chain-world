       The episode is complete after 26 weeks.

       **Summary of the run:**
       - Started with 80 units on hand against ~20 units/week baseline demand
       via the spot supplier on Suez.
       - Weeks 0–5: steady baseline replenishment; demand began drifting up
       toward 30+/week.
       - Weeks 6, 10–14: hit two severe, multi-week destination-port
       congestion events (berth_wait up to 16) that drove heavy demurrage and
       stockout costs; responded by halting new sea orders into the congested
       queue and leaning on air-expedite (capped 20/wk at 15/unit) to bypass
       the port directly.
       - Week 14–18: navigated a Suez/Red Sea disruption scare — correctly
       identified the first alarm as ambiguous, then confirmed a real
       disruption when counts stayed depressed and the bulletin escalated to
       "BREAKING," and shifted sourcing to the Cape route until the lane
       reverted to normal.
       - Week 19–20: detected spot supplier degradation directly (fill rate
       crashed to 0.13, audit reported "slipping," OTIF fell to 86%) and
       migrated the sourcing relationship to the reliable "qualified" supplier
       via a short-term contract, then lapsed the now-unproductive spot
       contract to stop paying dual-source overhead.
       - Weeks 21–26: demand cooled into a decline; held off on further orders
       once inventory position comfortably exceeded remaining horizon needs,
       avoiding wasted holding/shipping costs as the episode wound down.

       Final state at week 26: 42 units on hand, 30 still in transit (arriving
       after the horizon, a minor unavoidable tail), no open stockouts in the
       last several weeks, contracts cleanly resolved to qualified as primary.
       The two costliest stretches were the port-congestion weeks (demurrage +
       stockout + heavy air-freight bills) and the spot-supplier failure —
       both were identified from the signals as they emerged and mitigated
       (air bridge, route switch, supplier switch) rather than ridden out
       passively.
