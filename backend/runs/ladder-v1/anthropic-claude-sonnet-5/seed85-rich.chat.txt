========================== WRAP-UP (episode over) ==========================

AGENT
       The episode has concluded after 26 weeks.

       **Summary of how the horizon played out:**

       - **Weeks 0–4:** Steady-state replenishment at ~20 units/week via
       Suez+spot, matching baseline demand. Cheap and stable.
       - **Weeks 5–8:** A genuine Suez Canal disruption hit (grounded vessel
       blocking the canal). I confirmed it via briefing/audit, signed a
       qualified contract as a reliable fallback, shifted new orders to
       Cape+qualified to avoid canal exposure, paid a surcharge on one
       diverted shipment, and used air expediting to bridge on-hand gaps.
       Costly weeks, but avoided worse stockout/crisis-rate exposure.
       - **Weeks 9–13:** Lane reopened; freight rates crashed, so I locked in
       a cheap freight rate for 4 weeks and resumed cheaper Suez+spot
       sourcing. Spot showed a "slipping" wobble that briefly recovered.
       - **Weeks 14–19:** Spot degraded for real (one order shipped 0 units),
       so I abandoned spot entirely and moved all sourcing to qualified
       despite the premium. Demand also drifted upward (structural lift toward
       ~25-30/wk vs initial 20/wk baseline), causing a stockout when I under-
       ordered against the old baseline.
       - **Weeks 20–26:** Demand climbed further, and severe, sustained port
       congestion set in (berth_wait rising to 14-19), generating heavy
       demurrage on the large in-transit pipeline. I leaned on air-expediting
       most weeks to bypass the jammed port, stopped placing new sea orders
       (since they'd just queue and accrue more demurrage without arriving
       before horizon end), and rode out the existing pipeline. A second Suez
       incident broke in the final week, but with only one week left it no
       longer mattered.

       **Key lessons visible in the run:** the biggest costs came from the
       demand regime shift (underestimated the upward drift, causing a
       stockout) and the late-horizon port congestion (demurrage stacked up
       since in-transit inventory couldn't be diverted once committed). The
       Suez disruption in weeks 5-8 was handled reasonably well by diverting
       to Cape+qualified and using air-expedite to cover gaps. Lapsing the
       spot contract in week 22 correctly cut losses on a supplier that had
       gone unreliable.

       The episode is now complete — no further actions are possible.
